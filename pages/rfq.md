## What RFQ does

A request-for-quote auction sits between someone who wants to trade and the desks that want to fill the trade. When a trader asks for a price, Kamino opens an auction scoped to that exact order, broadcasts the opportunity to its onboarded market makers, and collects their bids. Kamino ranks the bids by output, selects the best one, verifies it, and co-signs the winning transaction to enforce the safety property. The trader signs and submits, and the fill settles atomically.

This is how illiquid assets get a tight, executable price. A tokenized treasury bill, a private-credit token, a fund share, a newly listed RWA — instruments a desk prices off its own inventory because no continuous on-chain book exists — get filled at a price a real desk is willing to honor, the instant the order is placed. The quote comes from inventory, on demand, so the asset is tradable from listing day with a desk standing behind every fill.

RFQ runs as a first-class route inside Kamino's execution stack, so every swap on Kamino already shops the solver network alongside the public venues and takes whichever returns more. Traders get solver pricing as the default; integrators get it through one API call.

## How a fill works

Five steps, start to finish, sub-second:

1. **The order opens an auction.** A trader requests a quote for a specific pair and size. Kamino creates an opportunity scoped to that order and starts a short bidding window.
2. **Solvers bid.** Onboarded market makers receive the opportunity and submit signed fill transactions priced from their own inventory. Each bid is a complete, executable transaction.
3. **Every bid is verified.** Each incoming bid is checked by independent simulation to confirm it executes cleanly and returns what it promises before it can win. Bids that fail verification are dropped.
4. **The best bid wins and is hardened.** Kamino picks the highest-output bid, then re-verifies the winner against a strict safety property: the only balances that can move are the trader's input and output, and the trader's wallet itself is never put at risk. A quote has to clear that check before it reaches the trader.
5. **The fill settles.** Kamino co-signs the verified transaction to bind the safety property, the trader signs to authorize the funds leaving their own wallet, and the fill settles in one atomic step. The trade completes in full or does not happen at all, so the trader is never exposed to a partial settlement.

The trader signs only a transaction that has already been simulated, hardened to move nothing but the input and output, and co-signed by Kamino to hold the solver to its quote.

## Price discovery for fixed-rate credit

The same competitive-quoting logic that prices an illiquid token prices term credit. A borrower posts an order — debt token, maximum rate, minimum term, amount — and lenders compete to supply it. The rate the borrower clears at is the market-clearing price for credit at that term, set live by competing lenders. Quote by quote across every duration, that competition is what forms an on-chain yield curve. ## Limit orders

A trader who wants to fill at a specific target price places a limit order. The input token is escrowed on-chain and the order stands until it executes or the trader cancels it. Fills happen at the rate the trader set, with zero slippage on execution and no platform fee.

Limit orders are filled by searchers competing in an auction to execute them. Two things flow back to the trader from that competition:

- **Surplus.** When a fill produces more output than the trader's minimum, the extra goes to the trader.
- **Tips.** The winning searcher pays the trader directly as part of executing the order.

Orders fill in full or incrementally as liquidity arrives, and the trader can cancel an unfilled order at any time to reclaim the escrowed input. Across the first $200M in limit-order volume, that competition has returned nearly $98,500 to traders in surplus and tips. ## Economics

- **Kamino takes nothing on the fill.** RFQ and limit orders carry no Kamino platform fee and no take on the spread. A fill costs the on-chain transaction fee and the price the winning quote returns; the protocol earns zero on RFQ flow.
- **Solvers earn the spread, bounded by competition.** A market maker's margin is the spread it quotes, and because the auction selects the highest-output bid, any desk that widens its spread loses the order to one that quotes tighter. Competition for the flow is what holds the spread down to what the asset and size can bear.
- **Issuers pay nothing to be tradable.** There is no listing or maintenance fee to make an asset quotable through RFQ. The desks fund the liquidity by quoting for the inventory they want.
- **Traders keep the upside.** Surplus above a limit order's minimum and searcher tips both flow back to the trader.
