# Institutional framing lens — the concreteness mandate

**Purpose.** Every product, use-case, and audience page must make Kamino's value *legible to a real institutional buyer*. No abstract capability-speak. A senior wealth-management decision-maker should land on a page and immediately understand: **what they can do, how it works operationally, what they get, and why Kamino specifically.**

**The clarity bar — the Stripe Payments standard.** A buyer should read any Kamino page the way a company reads Stripe's Payments pages: within a couple of minutes they know the value, understand how it works, and know exactly what to do next — act, or read deeper. Speak the buyer's language (capital markets, operations, risk, custody, compliance), show the mechanism plainly, give proof, and always offer the next step. MS is the caliber of player we pitch — write to that level.

**Confidentiality (hard rule).** This lens is internal build guidance. The website copy must **never** name a specific institution, contact, or private conversation. Keep scenarios general — "a wealth manager," "an institution whose clients hold tokenized funds" — and make them concrete.

## The buyer to write for
A large wealth-management institution exploring on-chain. It has client assets (funds, tokenized funds, private-company shares) and idle client cash, and it's deciding what to actually do. Two decision-makers must be satisfied on every page:

- **The economics / operations decision-maker** — cares about unit economics, operational lift versus their current workflow, controls, reconciliation, operational support and escalation paths when something moves, client impact, and franchise risk. Speaks finance and operations, has no interest in DeFi philosophy.
- **The architecture / platform decision-maker** — cares about sub-custody integration, KYB and permissioning plumbing, wallet architecture, oracle feeds, and how Kamino fits alongside what they already run without becoming a third integration headache.

## What they need answered, concretely, on the relevant pages
- **Pricing the assets.** "Getting pricing from an oracle perspective is really hard." → **Scope** must answer this in operational terms — how it prices hard-to-value, illiquid, NAV-based, and private assets safely, the guards that protect against bad prints, and the track record — stated concretely.
- **Borrow against held assets without selling.** The custody-preserving path: assets stay in a custodied / locked account, Kamino records and prices them, clients draw stablecoin credit against them, with clear liquidation and redemption SLAs and full permissioning. (**Custodial Borrowing + RWA Liquidity + RFQ + Permissioning + Scope.**)
- **A trusted cash-alternative yield.** A permissioned vehicle an institution can offer its clients for yield on idle stablecoin balances, with transparency and controls — aimed at being the trusted brand for non-crypto-native idle cash. (**Lending Vaults / Meta-Vaults / Institutional Yield / Permissioned Vaults / Yield API.**)
- **Bringing private holdings on-chain.** Tokenizing private-company or fund holdings and standing up markets for the people who hold them — where the transfer-restriction and eligibility logic lives, and how liquidity and credit get created. (**Markets + Permissioned assets + Scope + RWA Liquidity + Curation.**)
- **One way Kamino differentiates — use sparingly, and only where a page genuinely calls for it.** Kamino ships the in-house oracle (Scope), the liquidation engine, the routing, and hands-on integration as working, audited, operated infrastructure, so a partner configures a venue on top of components that already run in production. The partner — or the curator it chooses — keeps control of the strategy, the risk parameters, and the access rules. *Guardrails:* mention this only where a page genuinely calls for it; do not stamp it on every page, do not make it the spine of the offering, and do not write it as "one team does X / one operator does Y" over and over. **Never claim Kamino runs a client's risk management or does the work for them** — Kamino supplies and secures the infrastructure; the client or curator sets the parameters and manages the risk. **Kamino is non-custodial infrastructure and is not a credit counterparty:** never call Kamino a "counterparty" or imply it guarantees principal or stands behind losses (that word is for the actual borrowers, lenders, and market makers). Express it positively, never name a competitor, and never use "not just plumbing" or any "not X — Y" / "more than X" construction.
- **Control and compliance as operational facts** — permissioning (who can do what, per wallet / asset / operation), KYB, whitelisted liquidators, the audit and security record — stated plainly.

## The test for every page (apply in review and in Phase C)
Could the two decision-makers above read this page and (1) understand exactly what they can do, (2) see how it works operationally, (3) grasp the concrete value, and (4) understand why Kamino specifically? If any answer is "it reads abstract," rewrite with the concrete job, the concrete mechanism, the concrete value, and why Kamino specifically — opening or anchoring the value in a concrete scenario where it helps, without naming any institution.
